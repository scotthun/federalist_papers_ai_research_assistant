## Digest: Academic RAG abstention literature
### Claims
- Self-RAG uses learned "reflection tokens" (Retrieve/IsREL/IsSUP/IsUSE) generated by the model itself, not a raw similarity threshold — source: arXiv:2310.11511, 2023-10-17, confidence: high
- CRAG uses a separately trained T5-large evaluator outputting a confidence score in [-1,1], with dataset-specific thresholds routing to Correct/Incorrect/Ambiguous actions — a trained classifier + threshold, not raw embedding similarity — source: arXiv:2401.15884, 2024-01-29, confidence: high
- RAGAS's Faithfulness metric is LLM-based claim verification against context, not embedding similarity; only Answer Relevance uses embedding similarity (and that's for a different purpose — question-reconstruction similarity, not retrieval confidence) — source: arXiv:2309.15217, 2023-09-26, confidence: high
- RALMs are poorly calibrated: similarity/relevance-based signals alone are insufficient to trigger correct abstention; models both over-refuse answerable questions and over-answer unanswerable ones — source: arXiv:2509.01476, 2025-09, confidence: medium
- Google Research "Sufficient Context" study: adding retrieved context can paradoxically REDUCE willingness to abstain — Gemma hallucinated on 10.2% of questions with no context vs. 66.1% with insufficient-but-present context. Proposes an LLM-based sufficiency classifier instead of similarity scores. **This directly undercuts "high similarity = safe to answer."** — source: arXiv:2411.06037 (ICLR 2025) / research.google blog 2025-05-14, confidence: high
- 2025 Google/Oxford study: instruction-tuned LLMs answer falsely rather than abstain 54.3% of the time when context is insufficient; conclude inference-time thresholding alone is not a full fix, training-level intervention needed for full reliability — source: arXiv:2512.23836, 2025-12-29, confidence: medium
